Guide

Four things the chart on the bag doesn’t tell you.

01

Weigh it, don’t scoop it

A cup is a volume, not a weight. The same 240 ml holds 86 g of the puppy kibble but only 77 g of the adult. Kitchen scales cost less than a bag of food and take the guesswork out.

02

Puppies go by adult weight

The puppy chart is indexed by what your dog will weigh full grown, not what they weigh today. Miniatures land around 4–5 kg and standards around 9 kg; your breeder or vet can narrow it down.

03

Treats come out of the total

Anything that isn’t kibble still counts. Keep treats to roughly a tenth of the day’s calories and take that off the measured amount rather than adding it on top.

04

Watch the dog, not just the number

You should feel ribs without pressing and see a waist from above. Extra weight is hard on a dachshund’s long back, so treat the chart as a starting point and re-check the fit every few weeks.

About

Every number here is transcribed from Royal Canin Dachshund packaging — the Puppy bag for 2 to 10 months, the Adult bag from 11 months on. Nothing is invented. Where your dog falls between two printed columns the amount is read proportionally between them, and the working under each result shows exactly which cells it came from.

The two bags aren’t interchangeable. Puppy kibble is 86 g per 240 ml cup at 3887 kcal/kg; the adult is 77 g at 3726 kcal/kg. Each life stage uses its own figures, so a number from one bag doesn’t transfer to the other.

This is a convenience tool for these two specific foods, not veterinary advice. If your dog is neutered, pregnant, unwell or on a different food — or if what you see in their body condition disagrees with the chart — your vet’s advice wins.
